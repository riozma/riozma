This week Trimble, Bentley and Google all showed AI that works with building models, and last week Qonic let AI assistants export IFC. Under all of this sits an old, unglamorous question: when a model leaves one program and arrives in another, what actually arrives? The answer, in most projects, is IFC.

What IFC is

IFC, the Industry Foundation Classes, is the open data format for building information models. It is maintained by buildingSMART International, a non-profit organisation, and standardised as ISO 16739. Every major BIM program can write and read it, so an architect working in Archicad, an engineer in Revit or Tekla and a checker in Solibri can exchange models without owning the same software. Version IFC 4.3 extended the format from buildings to infrastructure such as roads, railways and bridges.

An IFC file describes elements as typed objects: a wall is an IfcWall, a slab an IfcSlab. Each element has a geometry, properties grouped in property sets (for example fire rating or load-bearing yes/no), and relationships: which storey it belongs to, which opening it contains, which material layers it is made of.

The key point: IFC transports results, not the design logic

A wall in Revit or Archicad "knows" that it should follow the storey height and wrap around a corner. In IFC it arrives as a wall with a shape and properties, but the rules that produced the shape stay behind. That is why IFC is excellent for coordination, checking and handing over, and much weaker for continuing to design in another program. Most practical problems come from three places:

  • Mapping: the sending program must know which IFC class each of its objects becomes. A custom family that is not mapped often arrives as IfcBuildingElementProxy, a generic "something" that no checking rule recognises.
  • Properties: only the properties selected for export travel, and they must have the names the receiver expects. "FireRating", "Brandschutz" and "EI60" in a comment field are three different things to a machine.
  • Geometry: a shape can be written as a clean extrusion or as thousands of triangles. Both look the same on screen, but the second is heavy and can no longer be measured or edited sensibly.

How good projects handle it

StepWhat it means
Agree the exchangeAt the start, the team agrees which IFC version and which Model View Definition to use, which classes and properties are required, and at which project phases models are exchanged. In Switzerland this is typically written down in the project's BIM execution plan.
Write requirements as rulesWith IDS, the Information Delivery Specification, such requirements become machine-readable: "every IfcWall that is load-bearing must have a fire rating". A checking tool can then test every delivery automatically.
Test earlyExport a small test model in week one, open it in the receiving programs and in a viewer, and fix the mapping before hundreds of elements depend on it.
Check every deliveryEach incoming model is checked against the agreed rules before it is used for coordination, quantities or calculations.

Why this matters even more with AI

AI assistants that read and edit models, from the MCP servers in Revit and Allplan to agents in Qonic or Trimble Connect, depend on exactly the information IFC carries: clear classes, consistent properties and relationships. The new benchmarks show the gap clearly. In the BIM-Edit benchmark, which asks models to change IFC models from plain-language instructions, the best model reached an average of about 49%, and almost no task was solved completely. An element that arrives as a proxy with its fire rating hidden in a comment is invisible to a person running a rule, and just as invisible to an AI.

What it means for us as future planners

Treat the export as part of your deliverable, not as a button at the end. Know which class and properties your elements need, open your own IFC in a free viewer before you send it, and ask the receiving engineer what they need to calculate or check. Planners who can set up a clean exchange are the ones whose models other people, and soon AI tools, can actually use.
