Stoa raises money for AI that cites its sources, a London office is built from 78% reused steel

Helsinki start-up Stoa raised €3.5M for an AI that answers site questions from a project's documents with a link to the source, and Linarc's assistant Lia now compares drawing revisions and drafts submittal registers. In London, software built on Tekla matched reclaimed beams to a new office frame. Taisei sets up a ¥24bn fund for robotics and AI, PSI demolishes Switzerland's first research reactor building, and a London conference asks who pays for construction robots. Our focus: data formats beyond IFC.

  • Stoa raises €3.5M for an AI that answers site questions from drawings, schedules and models, with a citation to the source (Tech.eu)
  • Linarc's AI assistant 'Lia' indexes plan sets, compares revisions and drafts submittal registers, with a confidence level and a source for every item (Linarc)
  • London office reuses about 78% of its steel frame, matched to the new design by software built on Tekla (Construction Briefing)
  • Japanese contractor Taisei sets up a ¥24bn venture fund for AI, robotics and digital-twin start-ups worldwide (Pegasus Tech Ventures (PR Times, Japanese))
  • Switzerland's first research reactor building comes down: 180,000 measuring points before the excavators could start (Paul Scherrer Institut)
  • Cornell tool records how builders adapt reclaimed materials on site, and shows the gap to the plan in 3D (Cornell Chronicle)
  • At London's first construction robotics conference: a bricklaying robot at 800 bricks a day, and the question of who pays for it (Robotics & Automation News)
  • Volvo CE builds Trimble's excavator guidance directly into its cab display, without extra hardware (Construction Briefing)
